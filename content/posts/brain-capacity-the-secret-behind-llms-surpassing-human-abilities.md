Today’s LLMs far surpass human capabilities across a wide range of domains. This naturally leads us to wonder: why can they perform better than humans? It is because they possess “brain capacity” several orders of magnitude greater than that of humans.

People generally use IQ to measure how intelligent someone is. But there are two other important factors: brain capacity and ways of thinking. Only by considering all three together can we truly assess a person’s intelligence. At present, LLMs appear to fall short of the brightest humans in IQ and lag far behind humans in how they think. Yet their immense brain capacity compensates for both shortcomings, enabling them to exhibit remarkable intelligence.

## 1. Intelligence

There is little doubt that LLMs will continue to grow more intelligent and will eventually surpass humans by a wide margin. For now, however, they still have a notable weakness: their answers are often comprehensive without being truly insightful. When faced with complex tasks, they can become trapped in a local solution, repeatedly exploring the same limited area instead of breaking through to a better approach.

## 2. Ways of Thinking

This is where today’s LLMs are weakest, which is why they need carefully designed agent architectures and orchestrated workflows. Humans can step outside an immediate predicament and reconsider it from a higher level. We continually shift perspectives, reframe problems, and move toward more abstract ways of thinking. This capacity has given rise to politics, philosophy, and aesthetics.

## 3. Brain Capacity

Intelligence and brain capacity are not the same thing. They can compensate for each other to some extent, but reaching the highest level in certain fields requires both. Consider ICPC contestants, Go masters, and mathematicians: exceptional intelligence is essential, but so is the capacity to hold and manipulate a vast amount of information at once. Brain capacity is not memory. In computing terms, intelligence is like the CPU, brain capacity is like RAM—the workspace available for active thought—and memory is like a hard drive.

The pace of scientific and technological progress has always been constrained by the limits of the human brain. Yet brain capacity is precisely where LLMs hold their greatest advantage. That is why their performance can be so astonishing.
